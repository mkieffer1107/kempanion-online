## 1. Traditions & lore

### 1.1 Spirit and game day

**Go Gators and the Gator Chomp**
- The move: arms stacked and clapped together like jaws, timed to the band's two-note "Jaws" riff.
- Origin: pep-band members copied a hand motion Mississippi State cheerleaders made to the *Jaws* theme on Sept 26, 1981. They debuted it at home vs Maryland on Oct 10, 1981.
- It was first called "Gator Jaws" and renamed over film-trademark worries ([Deseret](https://www.deseret.com/2022/9/2/23331348/florida-gator-traditions-chomp-i-wont-back-down-sec-the-swamp), [FloridaGators.com](https://floridagators.com/news/2014/4/22/27992)). One outlier source says 1992 `[CONFLICT]`.
- Game notes: the *Jaws* theme is copyrighted, so write an original two-note chomp motif. Pigs doing the Chomp with hooves is free comedy.

**The "Gator Bait" chant is retired. Never use it.** `[SENSITIVE]`
- In 2020 UF's president dropped it, citing historic racist imagery tied to the phrase. The Chomp itself continues.
- I found this in [an opinion outlet's recap](https://thefederalist.com/2025/04/09/florida-gators-won-march-madness-but-lost-their-iconic-cheer-to-the-woke-mob/); the decision was widely reported in June 2020.

**Albert & Alberta**
- The first mascot was a live alligator named Albert, donated in 1957 by reptile showman Ross Allen. He was later released into Lake Alice.
- One live Albert died after people fed him: a necropsy found a pop bottle blocking his gut. The specimen is dated Oct 1974, and the skull is on display at the Florida Museum ([Florida Museum](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/100-years/object/alberts-skull)).
- The costumed Albert dates from 1970. Alberta arrived in 1984 per Wikipedia, or 1986 per UF athletics and the Alligator `[CONFLICT]` ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_and_Alberta_Gator), [Gators mascot history](https://floridagators.com/sports/2015/12/10/_spirit_mascots_history_)).
- A 2015 redesign made the costumes lighter for the heat. Sports Illustrated's On Campus ranked Albert the No. 1 mascot in 2007.

**The Swamp**
- Ben Hill Griffin Stadium was built in 1930 as Florida Field. It was renamed in 1989 and has been "Steve Spurrier-Florida Field" since 2016. Official capacity is 88,548.
- Spurrier named it "The Swamp", most sources say in 1992 (some say 1991) `[CONFLICT]`.
- UF marketing later added the tagline "Only Gators get out alive" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Hill_Griffin_Stadium)).

**Gator Walk and the band**
- About 2 to 2.5 hours before kickoff, fans line the route as the team arrives by bus and walks in. Band members play along the route.
- The marching band is called "The Pride of the Sunshine". In the pregame show they spell "Gators" in cursive script ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pride_of_the_Sunshine), [SDS](https://www.saturdaydownsouth.com/florida-football/what-makes-game-day-at-the-swamp-a-destination-for-every-college-football-fan)).

**Mr. Two Bits**
- George Edmondson Jr. began leading the "Two Bits" cheer on Sept 24, 1949, vs The Citadel. He retired in 2008 and died on July 2, 2019, at age 96 or 97 `[CONFLICT]`.
- A guest "Mr. Two Bits" now leads the cheer pregame ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Two_Bits), [Gators](https://floridagators.com/news/2019/8/5/football-gators-final-cheer-for-mr-two-bits)).

**End of the 3rd quarter**
- Fans link arms and sway to "We Are the Boys from Old Florida".
- Since Oct 7, 2017, days after Tom Petty died, the stadium then sings his "I Won't Back Down". At night games it becomes a sea of phone lights ([Gators 2017](https://floridagators.com/news/2017/10/5/football-gators-to-honor-gainesville-legend-music-icon-tom-petty-on-saturday), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Are_the_Boys_from_Old_Florida)).
- Do not reproduce lyrics or melodies of either song.

**Homecoming and Gator Growl**
- The first Homecoming parade was in 1916, led by the band. Themed floats date from 1948. UF calls it the nation's largest student-run parade `[CLAIM]`.
- Gator Growl is run by the student honor society Florida Blue Key. UF News dates it to 1923; it is often counted from 1932 `[CONFLICT]`.
- Early Growls ended with a bonfire that freshmen had to feed with their body weight in firewood. That was dropped in the early 1950s.
- Jerry Seinfeld opened in 1988, and the local band Sister Hazel debuted "All for You" there in 1997 ([UF News](https://news.ufl.edu/2023/10/100-years-of-gator-growl/)).
- **2026:**
  - Homecoming was Oct 9–10, with no classes.
  - The 103rd Gator Gallop fun run was Oct 9.
  - Gator Growl was Oct 9 at the O'Connell Center, headlined by Nikki Glaser ([UF calendar](https://calendar.hr.ufl.edu/event/gator-growl-2026/), [RunSignup](https://runsignup.com/Race/FL/Gainesville/UFHomecomingAndGatorGrowlGatorGallopFunRun)).
  - The Homecoming game was a 19–38 loss to South Carolina, so it is a sore subject.

**2026 home-game designations** ([SI](https://www.si.com/college/florida/football/florida-gators-home-game-designations-2026-football-season-01ks60qwb21h)):

### 1.2 Campus landmarks

**Century Tower and the carillon** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Century_Tower_(University_of_Florida)))
- A 157-ft modified Collegiate Gothic tower, begun in 1953 and finished in 1956 (some sources say 1953) `[CONFLICT]`.
- It marks UF's 1853 roots in the East Florida Seminary and memorializes students killed in World War I and World War II.
- Bells:
  - 49 bells bought in 1976 with student fees and dedicated in 1979.
  - Expanded to 61 bells in 2003, covering 5 octaves and weighing about 57,760 lb in all. The biggest bell (the bourdon) is about 7,000 lb.
  - Played with 61 hand batons and 25 foot pedals, up 194 steps.
  - North America has fewer than 200 carillons, and Florida has four.
- The tower chimes automatically every quarter hour from 8 am to 8 pm, using a melody unique to UF: "Florida Chimes" by Budd Udell.
- Students play live Mon–Fri 12:35–12:50 and 4:55–5:10 pm while classes are in session, plus Sunday studio recitals. Listeners are told to stand at least 100 ft away ([weekly program](https://arts.ufl.edu/site/assets/files/68430/5_september_8-12_2025.pdf), [UF calendar](https://calendar.hr.ufl.edu/event/annual-halloween-spooktacular-presented-by-the-uf-carillon-and-organ-studios/)) `[DATED 2025]`.
- Music tip: the Westminster Quarters chime is public domain `[GENERAL]`; Udell's chime may not be. Write an original one.

**Plaza of the Americas**
- The central quad, shaded by live oaks. Its landscape plan dates to Frederick Law Olmsted Jr. in 1925, and it is part of the campus historic district on the National Register.
- It works as campus's unofficial free-speech forum, and students study, nap and picnic under the oaks ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_of_the_Americas_(Gainesville,_Florida))).
- Hammocks are commonly seen, but I could not confirm UF's rules on them `[UNCERTAIN]`.

**Krishna Lunch** (status Oct 2026: off campus)
- *History:*
  - It began in 1971 after the founder of ISKCON (the Hare Krishna movement) visited Gainesville that July.
  - Lunch was served on the Plaza on weekdays, 11 am–1:30 pm, peaking at about 800–1,000 plates a day.
  - A $200k alumni-funded renovation named the Plaza's west corridor "Krishna Lunch Lane" ([ISKCON News](https://iskconnews.org/krishna-lunch-finds-new-ways-to-continue-serving-gainesville/), [Alligator 2012](https://www.alligator.org/r/3bd46523a6)).
- *Menu:*
  - Vegetarian and all-you-can-eat, with vegan versions on request.
  - Rice; beans and chickpeas cooked from dried; pasta (Spaghetti Wednesday later became penne); potatoes; salad drowned in a famous almond-based dressing; chili on Fridays; chocolate halava for dessert.
  - The last campus menu, on Feb 20, 2026, was rice, chili, cheesy potatoes and chocolate halava ([Alligator 2019](https://www.alligator.org/r/3675a68219), [Alligator 2013](https://www.alligator.org/r/0b6f6c738f), [WUFT 2026](https://www.wuft.org/heard-on-wuft-fm/2026-08-27/uf-ends-contract-allowing-krisha-lunch-on-campus-groups-adviser-says)).
- *Price:*
  - Always formally a donation. It went $3, then $4 (2007), $5 (2012), $6, and briefly $8 in early 2025, which drew backlash.
  - UF then pushed the group to describe meals as free with a suggested donation. People who couldn't pay could volunteer for a free plate ([Alligator Mar 2025](https://www.alligator.org/r/387d5d1658), [Alligator Jan 2026](https://www.alligator.org/article/2026/01/krishna-lunch-launches-online-fundraiser)).
- *Now:*
  - Meals come from Krishna House (214 NW 14th St, an address from 2013) `[DATED]`, through DoorDash and Grubhub, or as "Krishna To Go" preorders.
  - Same team, same recipes ([ISKCON News](https://iskconnews.org/krishna-lunch-finds-new-ways-to-continue-serving-gainesville/)).
- `[SENSITIVE]` No jokes about the accident. Keep the religious group respectful and use the lunch as nostalgia or a Krishna To Go item.

**Turlington Plaza and "The Potato"**
- A 10-ton chert boulder in front of Turlington Hall, nicknamed "The Potato". "Meet at the Potato."
- Its 1984 plaque says it is about 30 million years old; one source says 30,000 `[CONFLICT]`.
- It formed from Suwannee limestone, was found in a Hernando County quarry and was donated by Florida Crushed Stone ([Waymarking](https://www.waymarking.com/waymarks/WMXE1N_Turlington_Rock_Gainesville_FL)).
- I found no reliable source saying students paint it `[UNCERTAIN]`.

**The 34th Street Wall**
- A roughly 1,120-ft retaining wall the state Department of Transportation built in 1979 on SW 34th St.
- It is constantly repainted with birthdays, proposals and game wins; in places the paint is about 250 layers thick. It also has a Tom Petty memorial ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/34th_Street_Wall), [WUFT](https://www.wuft.org/news/2018/06/26/citizens-monument-34th-street-wall-showcases-gainesville-history/)).
- `[SENSITIVE]` Its permanent centerpiece memorializes five students murdered in 1990. Keep jokes away from it, and don't name the killer.

**Lake Alice**
- A campus wildlife sanctuary; the surrounding conservation area is about 130 acres, UF's largest `[UNCERTAIN acreage]`.
- It was once called "Jonah's Pond" and was probably renamed for a farmer's daughter `[UNCERTAIN]`.
- It is one of the few spots in town where you reliably see wild gators, and it doubles as a stormwater sponge ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Alice_(Gainesville,_Florida)), [WUSF](https://www.wusf.org/environment/2022-09-03/a-lake-named-alice-blooms-and-bureaucracy-imperil-the-university-of-floridas-landmark-campus-lake)).
- Feeding the gators is how Albert died.

**Bat barns at dusk** (2664 Museum Rd, beside Lake Alice)
- *Origin:*
  - After a 1987 fire at Johnson Hall, displaced bats moved into stadium bleachers, and they spooked crowds at track meets.
  - Athletics funded the first bat house in 1991. The bats ignored it for over three years and moved in in 1995.
  - Bat "barns" followed in 2010 and 2017. The original house was demolished in 2022 after the bats were moved before Hurricane Ian.
- *Colony:*
  - About 450,000–500,000 bats, mostly Brazilian free-tailed. Possibly the largest colony east of the Mississippi `[CLAIM]`.
  - The colony eats an estimated 2.5 billion insects a night.
  - Free-tails can fly up to 100 mph and forage about 30 miles.
- *Watching:*
  - They emerge 15–20 minutes after sunset; spring through early summer is best.
  - Calm nights above 65°F are ideal; rain, wind or cold keeps them in.
  - Don't stand under the barns (guano and urine).
- Sources: [bats.ufl.edu](https://bats.ufl.edu/about-the-houses), [plan your visit](https://bats.ufl.edu/plan-your-visit), [UF News 2022](https://news.ufl.edu/2022/08/bat-house-renovation/), [WUFT 2025](https://www.wuft.org/heard-on-wuft-fm/2025-10-30/audio-postcard-at-sunset-bats-fill-the-sky-at-ufs-lake-alice).

### 1.3 Invented or built here

**Gatorade (1965)**
- *Inventors:* a UF College of Medicine kidney specialist, Dr. J. Robert Cade, with Dana Shires, H. James Free and Alejandro de Quesada.
- *Origin:*
  - Assistant coach Dewayne Douglas asked why players barely urinated during games; the answer was that they had sweated it all out.
  - Wikipedia's Gatorade article instead credits head coach Ray Graves with the request, and Douglas's name and title vary `[CONFLICT]`.
- *Testing:* first tried on 10 freshman players in Sept 1965.
- *Recipe and name:*
  - The original recipe was water, sodium, sugar, potassium, phosphate and lemon juice.
  - The team avoided an "-aid" name because it implied a medicine.
- *Results and money:*
  - The 1967 Orange Bowl win over Georgia Tech got the credit.
  - A 1973 settlement gives UF 20% of royalties: over $150M by 2009.
  - The original commercial flavors were lemon-lime and orange.
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gatorade), [Robert Cade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Cade), [UF Research](https://research.ufl.edu/publications/explore/v08n1/gatorade.html).

**HiPerGator** (UF's supercomputer)
- *Where:* the UF Data Center on East Campus, 2008 NE Waldo Rd. The building opened in 2012, and HiPerGator 1.0 started in 2013 ([UF 2013](https://archive.news.ufl.edu/articles/2013/05/states-most-powerful-supercomputer-to-be-unveiled-at-uf.html)).
- *HiPerGator AI (2021):*
  - 140 NVIDIA DGX A100 nodes with 1,120 A100 GPUs.
  - Funded by the July 2020 $70M AI partnership: $25M from alumnus Chris Malachowsky, $25M in NVIDIA hardware, software and services, and $20M from UF ([CISE](https://cise.ufl.edu/uf-announces-70-million-artificial-intelligence-partnership-with-nvidia/)).
- *HiPerGator 4 (current):*
  - An NVIDIA DGX B200 "Blackwell" SuperPOD: 63 nodes with 8 GPUs each, 504 GPUs in all, about 30× the previous generation.
  - Installed by Sept 2025 and unveiled Oct 14, 2025.
  - Cost: $24M approved, $33M reported `[CONFLICT]`.
  - UF calls it the fastest university-owned supercomputer in the US `[CLAIM]`. It has about 7,000 users ([UF News Oct 2025](https://news.ufl.edu/2025/10/hipergator-4-unveiling/), [UF News 2024](https://news.ufl.edu/2024/12/hipergator-upgrade/)).
- Map gag: the first Sonny's BBQ (1968) was also on NE Waldo Rd.

**Tom Petty**
- A Gainesville native who worked briefly on UF's grounds crew but never enrolled.
- An Ogeechee lime tree on campus is called "the Tom Petty tree", though he said he didn't remember planting any trees.
- UF gave him an honorary doctorate after his death ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Petty), [FL marker](https://markers.flheritage.com/home/details/1277)).

### 1.4 Town and country

**"Hogtown"**
- Gainesville's lasting nickname. Hogtown was a neighboring early settlement, originally a Seminole village whose residents raised hogs, near today's 34th St & 8th Ave. The city annexed the site in 1961.
- Most historians treat it as separate from Gainesville rather than an earlier name `[CONFLICT]`.
- Hogtown Creek runs 5.7 miles through the city, and plenty of local businesses brand themselves "Hogtown" ([Hogtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogtown,_Florida), [Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogtown_Creek)).
- `[SENSITIVE]` Treat the Seminole history respectfully.

**Florida's first pigs**
- The state wildlife agency (FWC) says Florida's wild hogs may descend from Hernando de Soto's 1539 expedition `[hedged]`. Wild hogs now live in all 67 counties ([FWC](https://myfwc.com/wildlifehabitats/profiles/mammals/land/wild-hog/)).
- De Soto's army crossed Potano territory (today's Alachua County) in Aug 1539 and camped west of present-day Gainesville ([Potano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potano)). Reconstructions of the route vary.

### 3.3 Plain-language science (accurate enough to quote)

**Ischemia**
- Blood supply is too low for what a tissue needs, so it runs short of oxygen and glucose.
- The brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of the body's oxygen, and it has almost no reserve ([textbook excerpt](https://beckassets.blob.core.windows.net/product/readingsample/107870/9780521676892_excerpt_001.pdf)).
- In an untreated large stroke, about **1.9 million neurons die per minute**. That is a 2006 model estimate behind the slogan "time is brain" ([Saver 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16339467/)).
- The **core** is already dead. The surrounding **penumbra** is stunned but can be saved if blood flow returns in time; restoring flow can also cause some injury of its own.

**Perfusion**
- Blood actually moving through a tissue's capillaries.
- For the brain: cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP) = mean arterial pressure (MAP) minus intracranial pressure (ICP). Severe brain-injury care targets a CPP of about 60–70 mmHg ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10457629)).
- For the spinal cord: spinal cord perfusion pressure = MAP minus CSF pressure ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8168672)).

**Intracranial pressure (ICP)**
- The skull is a rigid box holding brain, blood and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF); this is the Monro–Kellie idea `[GENERAL]`.
- Normal adult ICP is about 5–15 mmHg.
- In severe brain injury, a sustained ICP above about 22 mmHg triggers treatment ([EMRA](https://www.emra.org/emresident/article/device-series-EVD)).

**CSF (cerebrospinal fluid)**
- About 150 mL is in an adult at any moment, with about 500 mL a day of turnover, so it is replaced about three times daily.
- It is mostly made by the choroid plexus, about 80% in the classical model ([StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519007/)).
- Gag: 500 mL is less than one 20-oz (591 mL) Gatorade bottle.

**External ventricular drain (EVD)**
- A neurosurgeon drills a burr hole, commonly at Kocher's point on the front-top of the skull. A catheter goes into the lateral ventricle (a fluid space in the brain), with its tip near the foramen of Monro.
- CSF drains to a bedside drip chamber. The system is zeroed at the **tragus** (the small ear flap), which lines up with the foramen of Monro.
- The height of the drip chamber sets the pressure at which CSF drains, and the drain also measures ICP.
- Used for hydrocephalus, bleeding into the ventricles and high ICP ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_ventricular_drain), [EMRA](https://www.emra.org/emresident/article/device-series-EVD)).

**Lumbar drain**
- A thin catheter in the CSF space of the lower back.
- Used for CSF leaks `[GENERAL]`, and during thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm repair, keeping CSF pressure below about 10 mmHg to improve blood flow to the spinal cord and prevent spinal-cord ischemia.
- The evidence for that use is mixed ([Cochrane](https://cochranelibrary.com/es/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD003635.pub3/full/zh_HANS)).
- Overdraining is dangerous `[GENERAL]`.

**Acute spinal cord injury:** the 2024 AO Spine/Praxis guideline suggests keeping MAP at least 75–80 and no higher than 90–95 mmHg for 3–7 days. It is a weak recommendation based on low-quality evidence ([Global Spine J](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10964896/)).

**EEG vs ECoG vs implanted arrays**
- **EEG** uses scalp electrodes and hears neurons "through the skull": blurry in space.
- **ECoG** puts grids or strips on the brain's surface, as in planning epilepsy surgery: a clearer signal, but more invasive.
- **The Utah array** is a 10×10 grid of needles (about 96 channels) on a roughly 4×4 mm chip, with needles 1–1.5 mm long. It records single neurons and is used in BrainGate brain-computer interfaces ([MD+DI](https://www.mddionline.com/neurological/neurosurgery-researchers-have-microelectrodes-on-the-brain), [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8981395)).

**Brain-computer "microchips"**
- Neuralink's N1 has 1,024 electrodes on 64 flexible threads, placed by its R1 robot.
- Its first human patient, Noland Arbaugh, was implanted in Jan 2024 at Barrow Neurological Institute. Many threads later pulled back (estimates go up to about 85%), and software changes recovered performance ([Fierce](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/neuralink-implants-brain-computer-chip-second-patient-media-report), [PopSci](https://www.popsci.com/health/neuralink-wire-detachment/)).
- The **Aug 28, 2020 pig demo** ([CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2020/08/28/tech/elon-musk-neuralink), [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/computers/neuralink-brain-implant-pig-demonstration-elon-musk)):
  - Gertrude's implant read signals from her snout.
  - Dorothy had hers removed to show it could be taken out.
  - Joyce had no implant.

**Deep brain stimulation (DBS):** electrodes placed in deep brain targets for Parkinson's and tremor. UF and the Fixel Institute are major DBS centers.

### 3.4 Pigs in neuroscience (self-aware material)

- **Brain:** a pig brain is about 180 g against a human's 1,300 g. Its cortex is folded like ours (gyrencephalic) and over 60% white matter, which makes pigs a bridge between rodent and primate models ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6142048)).
- **Spinal cord:**
  - Researchers at the University of British Columbia built a Yucatan minipig spinal-injury model (a T10 contusion), scored on a pig walking scale called PTIBS (Lee et al., *J Neurotrauma* 2013).
  - Cord width: rat 3.2 mm, pig 7.0 mm, human 8.3 mm ([review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6129752)).
- **Organs:**
  - A gene-edited pig heart went into David Bennett at the University of Maryland on Jan 7, 2022. He died March 8, 2022 ([PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/science/in-a-first-u-s-surgeons-transplant-a-genetically-modified-pig-heart-into-a-human-patient)).
  - A pig kidney went into Richard Slayman at Massachusetts General Hospital on March 16, 2024. He died in May 2024; the hospital said there was no indication the transplant caused it ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2024/05/11/metro/mass-man-who-received-pig-kidney-transplant-dies)).
- **Oversight gag:** animal research is reviewed by an Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) and guided by the "3Rs": replace, reduce, refine `[GENERAL]`.
