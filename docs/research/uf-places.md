# Kempanion Online: UF campus background reference sheet

Compiled 2026-10-30 for Kempa AI's visual-novel backgrounds (16-bit / PC-98 pixel art with stylized sakura).

**How the photos were checked (2026-10-30):** this sheet lists 54 photos.
- **File pages:** I fetched each Commons file page and every one returned HTTP 200. The license and author come from that page's metadata and license templates.
- **Thumbnails:** every `upload.wikimedia.org` thumbnail link below returned HTTP 200 with an image content type. Links are 1280 px wide unless marked 1920 or 3840.
- **Originals:** these URLs come straight from the Commons API (`imageinfo.url`), but only one was confirmed by direct fetch (Malachowsky Hall, HTTP 200). After that, Wikimedia started returning HTTP 429 and asked automated clients to use standard thumbnail sizes. Use the thumbnail links in scripts and open originals in a browser.

**Framing rules that apply to every background**
- **Canvas:** draw at 640×360 (16:9, close to PC-98's 640×400) and scale up by whole numbers. Use a 16–32 colour palette per background, with ordered dithering for sky and foliage.
- **Text box:** the text box and name plate cover about the bottom quarter. Keep the horizon at 40–55% of the height and the landmark silhouettes in the top 60%. Keep the lower middle simple, because the 384×512 waist-up portraits sit there.
- **Phones held upright:** a 9:16 screen showing a 16:9 background at full height only shows the middle 32% of its width. Keep each location's identifying feature (tower crown, door, sign) in that central band, or draw a separate portrait crop.
- **Sakura:** use sakura as framing (branches coming in from the top corners, trees at the edges, petals in the foreground) or in place of crape myrtles and young street trees. Keep the live oaks with Spanish moss, plus the palms and pines, as the Florida signature. Never cover a landmark's outline.
- **Lighting variants:** make day, sunset and night versions of the main hangouts (Century Tower, Plaza, Lake Alice, dorm, Reitz Union, Midtown).

## 1. Century Tower
- **Where:** Century Tower, Newell Drive, in the middle of campus right beside the University Auditorium.
- **Look:** a 157-ft (48 m) red-brick bell tower in a "modified Collegiate Gothic" style, with cream cast-stone trim and buttress-like corner piers. The top stage is an open belfry with tall pointed arches and a pinnacled crown. Construction began in 1953. It marks the 100th anniversary of the East Florida Seminary and is a memorial to students and alumni who died in WWI and WWII. It holds a 61-bell carillon (49 bells bought in 1976 and dedicated in 1979, then 12 trebles added and rededicated in 2003). Around it are live oaks heavy with Spanish moss, palms, and the Auditorium's Gothic roofline. At night the 2022 photo shows the shaft floodlit warm orange with blue light inside the belfry.
- **16:9 framing:** a low 3/4 view from the Marston/CSE side. Put the tower on the right third with its crown about 10% below the top edge, live-oak branches and moss across the top left, sakura on the left edge, and the Auditorium gable in the lower right. For night, frame it between two brick piers (as in the Abair photo); this crop also works on upright phones.

## 2. Plaza of the Americas
- **Where:** Plaza of the Americas, the historic quad in the UF Campus Historic District. By OSM coordinates, Library West is to the north, Smathers Library and Peabody Hall to the east, Leigh Hall (the historic Chemistry Building) to the west, and the University Auditorium to the south with Century Tower just behind it.
- **Look:** a broad lawn crossed by straight sidewalks, shaded by large live oaks hung with Spanish moss and tall pines. It has benches and black lamp posts, and red-brick Collegiate Gothic buildings on every side. Frederick Law Olmsted Jr. planned it in 1925. It was named in 1931 at the first meeting of the Institute of Inter-American Affairs, when students planted 21 trees for the 21 invited countries. Wikipedia calls it UF's "unofficial forum". A 2016–17 renovation added landscaping, benches and sidewalks, so the photos below show the plaza before that work.
- **16:9 framing:** eye level from the Library West steps looking south. A central path leads to the Auditorium and Century Tower on the horizon (about 45% of the height), the oak canopy arches across the top, and sakura fill both sides in the middle distance. This is the classic "school courtyard at lunch" shot.

## 3. Turlington Hall and Turlington Plaza
- **Where:** Turlington Hall (completed 1977), 330 Newell Drive (OSM), next to Marston and the Auditorium.
- **Look:** a late-1970s modernist red-brick block. The upper floors overhang a recessed ground floor lined with brick piers. It has long dark window bands, a row of round porthole windows and a dark glazed band along the top. In front is a wide concrete plaza with black lamp posts, brick planters, oaks and pines. A large chert boulder, reportedly donated in 1984 and nicknamed "the Potato", is a well-known meeting spot.
- **16:9 framing:** a diagonal wide shot from the plaza. The building fills the top two-thirds of the width, open paving fills the bottom third for characters and the text box, the Potato sits on the left third as a midground prop, and sakura stand in the planters.

## 4. Marston Science Library
- **Where:** Marston Science Library (completed 1987), 444 Newell Drive (OSM). It is named for UF president Robert Q. Marston and sits right next to the CSE Building.
- **Look:** a five-storey postmodern red-brick building with stepped setbacks, long horizontal window bands and angled corners. Its courtyard has crape myrtles, oaks and brick planters. Between Marston and the CSE Building stands "Alachua" (1987, by John Raymond Henry), a bright-yellow tangle of steel beams that everyone calls "the French Fries". At night the entrance glows under a blue-lit skylight grid (2022 photo). The ground-floor "Collaboration Commons" (2014) has 3D scanning and printing, a multi-touch visualization wall and the MADE@UF lab for mobile apps and games, which suits Piggy 3.
- **16:9 framing:** a 3/4 courtyard view with the yellow French Fries as a bright accent on the left third, Marston's stepped facade on the right, and Century Tower's crown showing over the trees.

## 5. Library West
- **Where:** Library West, 1545 W. University Ave (OSM), at the north end of the Plaza of the Americas.
- **Look:** built in 1967. A $30M renovation (December 2003 to August 2006) doubled its capacity, added a Starbucks in the lobby and earned LEED Gold. Its red-brick and cream cast-stone front echoes the Collegiate Gothic neighbours with steep gables and a tall arched window bay, then joins a modern glass wing. Lawns, young oaks, palms and black lamp posts surround it. Students call it "Lib West" or "Club West", and the top two floors are quiet study.
- **16:9 framing:** the gabled arched bay on the left third, the glass wing receding to the right, and a lamp post in the foreground. For the inside, reuse the Gothic reading room in #24 or draw a modern carrel area (there is no free interior photo of Library West).

## 6. University Auditorium
- **Where:** University Auditorium, 333 Newell Drive (OSM). Designed by William A. Edwards and built 1922–24 in Collegiate Gothic style. James McGinley restored and expanded it in 1977. It is part of the historic district listed on the National Register in 1989.
- **Outside:** red brick with cream stone tracery, buttresses, tall traceried windows, a steep red roof with a slender spire, and green-patina roofs on the 1977 additions. Century Tower rises directly behind it.
- **Inside:** a hammerbeam roof modelled on London's Westminster Hall, with carved end-pieces showing scholars, musicians, engineers and football players. There are hanging chandeliers, side balconies, Art Deco "scholar" windows, and the pipes of the 1925 Skinner-built Anderson Memorial Organ above the stage.
- **16:9 framing:** outside, a broadside view from the southwest lawn with the tower on the right third (like the pano below). Inside, look down the centre aisle toward the organ, with chandeliers across the top.

## 7. J. Wayne Reitz Union
- **Where:** J. Wayne Reitz Union, 686 Museum Road. It opened in 1967, and the "Make It Reitz" expansion (about 126,000 sq ft added) opened on 1 February 2016. Inside are food venues, a game room, a hotel, a movie theater and meeting rooms. It is across Museum Road from Malachowsky Hall.
- **Look:** two different faces. The 1967 modernist building has long horizontal bands, a colonnade, and terraces that step down to a grassy amphitheater on Liberty Pond. The 2016 north wing is a glass curtain wall with a framed, full-height glass entrance bay and white and grey panels.
- **16:9 framing:** the hangout scene looks at the 2016 glass entry with banners and plenty of sky for weather changes. For sunset scenes, show the curved grass terraces dropping toward the pond.

## 8. Ben Hill Griffin Stadium ("The Swamp")
- **Where:** Steve Spurrier–Florida Field at Ben Hill Griffin Stadium, on the north edge of campus. It is bounded by W. University Ave to the north, Gale Lemerand Drive to the west and Stadium Road to the south. It opened in 1930 with 22,000 seats and its official capacity is now 88,548. Steve Spurrier coined "The Swamp" in 1992.
- **Look:** a steep, partly sunken bowl of orange-and-blue seating with a tall press and suite tower on the west side. The outside is brick, with a large mascot logo on the southwest corner.
- **Status:** a $1.45B renovation was approved in mid-2026, with construction expected roughly 2027–2030 (sources differ on the start). The current look matches the 2024 photo.
- **16:9 framing:** an upper end-zone corner looking across the field diagonal, with the bowl wrapping around and sky in the top third. Leave off the logos and wordmarks.

## 9. Lake Alice and the UF Bat Houses
- **Where:** Lake Alice, a campus wildlife area along Museum Road. The bat houses are a trio of raised structures at the Field & Fork gardens, across Museum Road from the lake.
- **Lake:** a small, still lake ringed by cypress, live oaks dripping Spanish moss, palmettos and lily pads, with alligators, Florida softshell turtles and wading birds. A boardwalk on the north side runs through swamp forest to a viewing platform, and the Baughman Center (#23) stands on the southwest bank. At sunset the water mirrors an orange and pink sky.
- **Bat houses:** white, barn-shaped wooden houses on tall stilts in an open field (2018 photo). The original was built in 1991 and barns were added in 2010 and 2017. About 500,000 bats, mostly Brazilian free-tailed bats, stream out at twilight. In 2022 UF tried to move the colony; the plan was to attach small houses to the newest barn and possibly paint it darker, so the current look may differ from the photo.
- **16:9 framing:** for the lake, a low horizon at about 55% with the reflection filling the lower half (under the text box), silhouetted trees across the top, and sakura on the near bank at left. For the bats, a wide field with the houses on the horizon, a twilight sky gradient and a swarm of bat sprites.

## 10. Malachowsky Hall (Piggy 1's home)
- **Where:** Malachowsky Hall for Data Science & Information Technology (UF bldg 1024), 1889 Museum Road.
  - It sits across Museum Road from the Reitz Union, about 240 m away, and about 190 m east of the New Physics Building (#12). Piggy 1 and Piggy 3 are neighbours.
  - It opened on 3 November 2023: seven storeys, 263,440 sq ft, about $150M. The architect was Bohlin Cywinski Jackson with Walker Architects.
  - It is named for NVIDIA co-founder and UF alumnus Chris Malachowsky.
  - It is headquarters for CISE (which moved here from the CSE Building in 2023), ECE, the cybersecurity institute (FICS) and the Nelms Institute. It has maker spaces for VR, IoT and robotics, plus AI and robotics labs.
- **Look:** a skin of pale silver-grey folded aluminium panels with "origami" creases, punched with dark triangular and trapezoidal windows that tint themselves (electrochromic glass). There are full-height glass sections and a glazed ground floor. Unlike its neighbours it has no orange brick. The grounds have native grasses, brick-paver walks and black lamp posts. Inside, a vertical "commons" links the floors.

- **Where:** Baughman Center, on the southwest bank of Lake Alice, completed in 2000. It is a 1,500 sq ft non-denominational pavilion seating 96, plus a small administrative building. AIA Florida ranked it 3rd in its "Florida Architecture: 100 Years. 100 Places." list.
- **Look:** walls of Florida cypress grooved vertically to look like tree trunks, lots of windows, and a steep roof of yellow pine and copper. A Gothic-arched timber frame surrounds a golden maple door inlaid with cherry. Inside is a travertine floor, and the building is aligned to the summer-solstice sunrise and sunset. Oaks and palms frame it.
- **16:9 framing:** perfectly symmetrical. A straight path leads to the Gothic door at the centre (works on upright phones), the oak canopy frames the top, and sakura stand on both sides. Ideal for a confession scene.

## 24. Smathers Library (Library East), extra
- **Where:** Smathers Library, designed by W. A. Edwards and opened in 1926, on the east side of the Plaza of the Americas. It was added to the National Register in 1979 and renamed in 1991. The second-floor Special Collections Grand Reading Room has Hollis Holbrook's 1953 mural "History of Learning in Florida".
- **Look:** Collegiate Gothic red brick with cream limestone trim, gables and tall arched windows. Inside the reading room are dark timber trusses, globe pendant lamps, tall pointed windows and long reading tables, a ready-made "academy library" background.
- **16:9 framing:** inside, a one-point view down the reading tables toward the far window, with the truss rhythm across the top.

## 25. 34th Street Wall, extra
- **Where:** 34th Street Wall, a 1,120-ft concrete retaining wall along SW 34th Street (SR 121) at the edge of UF's golf course. The state transportation department built it in 1979.
- **Look:** the whole length is covered in community graffiti (proposals, birthdays, game wins, memorials), with about 250 layers of paint at the centre. It includes permanent memorial panels for the victims of the 1990 Gainesville murders and for Tom Petty.
- **16:9 framing:** a long side-scrolling strip. This is a good spot for a "paint a message" mini-event where the player's text appears on the wall.

## 26. Helyx Bridge (night), extra
- **Where:** Helyx Bridge, a pedestrian and bike overpass over US 441 (SW 13th St) at the 900 block, south of Archer Road. It is an old rail bridge rebuilt in 2012 as a nod to the nearby Health Science Center.
- **Look:** two undulating "DNA" ribbons (actually a pair of offset sine waves) lit by LEDs at night, glowing over the traffic below.
- **16:9 framing:** a low 3/4 view, with the glowing ribbon sweeping from the upper left to the right edge and car light trails underneath. Use it for Piggy 2 walking home from Shands at night.
